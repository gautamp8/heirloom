# Carl Sagan  

Carl Sagan’s archive highlights his awe at the universe and humility about humanity’s place in it. I chose materials that show his scientific wonder and gentle wisdom passed to his children and future listeners. His famous *Pale Blue Dot* text anchors this section, illustrating how he encouraged cosmic perspective (“deal more kindly,” cherish Earth【68†L520-L522】). The photos show Sagan at home and with his Mars mission work, giving a family feel to the scientist. The sealed letter uses his own words (“That’s here. That’s home. That’s us…”【18†L446-L454】) to comfort someone feeling small, and the framing letter addresses a future generation with his vision of humanity’s shared responsibility in the cosmos【68†L502-L510】【68†L520-L522】.

# Fred Rogers  

Fred Rogers’s archive pieces emphasize his messages of unconditional love and understanding. The notes include passages from his commencement speeches and conversations about love and service, highlighting how he taught self-worth and empathy (“everybody can do something”【54†L58-L61】). The photo captions are written as if by his family, placing him in warm, domestic scenes (building gingerbread houses, playing piano with his wife) to humanize this beloved figure. The sealed letter is a tender message for a scared or lonely child (adapted from Rogers’s words about inner goodness【54†L108-L110】). The framing letter uses real Rogers quotes to speak to a future child about self-acceptance, neighborly love, and making a difference【54†L58-L61】【54†L129-L132】【54†L102-L106】【53†L314-L319】.

# Mahatma Gandhi  

Gandhi’s materials focus on personal humility, service, and conscience, rather than his political speeches. The note captures include his famous “Talisman” guidance to care for the poorest【81†L31-L38】 and his creed of nonviolence (“Ahimsa is the first article of my faith… the last article of my creed.”【80†L402-L405】). The photos show Gandhi with his wife Kasturba and caring for a calf, grounding these lofty ideals in everyday compassion. The sealed letter (“When in doubt… recall the face of the poorest”【81†L31-L38】) is offered for moments of selfish confusion. The framing letter lines are drawn from the Talisman, posing Gandhi’s questions to a future reader about serving humanity【81†L31-L38】.
